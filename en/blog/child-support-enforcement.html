Enforcing child support: what to do when the other parent doesn't pay

13 October 2026 · Bianca Rohozneanu, Attorney at Law

If you have a court judgment ordering the other parent to pay child support, but they fail to comply, you can apply for enforcement of the child support obligation.

There is no need to wait for the situation to drag on indefinitely. Depending on the specific circumstances, unpaid child support can be recovered through enforcement proceedings.

How can child support be recovered?

A judicial enforcement officer (bailiff) may, under the conditions set by law, use measures such as:

  • garnishment of the debtor's income;
  • garnishment of bank accounts;
  • seizure of certain assets;
  • recovery of the outstanding amounts, together with any accessories owed.

What documents are needed?

Mainly, you will need:

  • the court judgment establishing the child support;
  • details of the periods and amounts that were not paid.

What happens if they don't pay despite a court judgment?

Failure to pay child support can have legal consequences, and it does not mean the obligation disappears. In certain situations, bad-faith non-payment may even carry criminal consequences, separately from the enforcement procedure.
